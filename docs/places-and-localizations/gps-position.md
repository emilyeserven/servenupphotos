# GPS position

A place can have a **GPS position**, written on the Places page as `latitude, longitude` in decimal degrees (such as `37.5796, 126.977`, which is what right-clicking a spot in Google Maps copies). It's saved as `latitude` and `longitude` numbers in the place file, left out when not set; saving refuses only one of them, or one off the globe.

Every GPS field (a place's, **Add a GPS position for …**, and a photo's custom one) also takes degrees, minutes and seconds with N, S, E or W, as Wikipedia writes them: `37°34′12″N 126°59′56″E`, `37° 34' 12.5" N, 126° 59' 56" E`, `33°51′S 151°12′E` (minutes and seconds optional), exiftool's `37 deg 34' 12.50" N`, and latitude and longitude in either order. Once what's typed or pasted reads as a whole position, the field rewrites it as decimal degrees (`37.57, 126.998889`, rounded to six places, about 10cm), so it's saved the same way as one copied from Google Maps (`decimalGps` in `lib/place-gps.ts`). A **Suggest a location** answer with its latitude and longitude written that way is read the same.

> **Frontend analogy:** it's an input mask that normalizes on change, like a phone field turning `(555) 123-4567` into `+15551234567` before it reaches form state. On the portfolio, a photo with no GPS position of its own (a [custom one](a-photos-places.md#custom-gps-position) or its EXIF one) goes on the map at its first place's position, or else a place that place is in, or else its next place's. The hover text still shows only a photo's own (custom or EXIF) position. Its map marker gets a softer border, as the spot is only near where the photo was taken, unless the place is ticked as an **Exact location** (such as a subway station or a museum, where its photos were taken right at its GPS position): then the marker keeps the same solid white border as a photo with its own GPS position. It's saved as `"exactLocation": true`, only on a place with a GPS position, and a place that uses the position of a place it's in is never exact.

The list's GPS column shows a filled crosshair for a place with its own GPS position, a plain one for a place that uses the position of a place it's in (hover for which), and an amber (!) warning on a place with neither when photos listing it have no EXIF GPS position and none of their places has one either, so they're missing from the map. Hovering the warning says how many photos; giving the place, or a place it's in, a position clears it.

> **Frontend analogy:** like a CSS fallback: `top: var(--exif-gps, var(--place-gps))`.
