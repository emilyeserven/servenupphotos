# A photo's places

On the Photo page and in Edit Photo, the title and comment are in one card and the photo's location in another: **Place**, then **City, state and country**. Under the **Place** title, a line says where the photo goes on the portfolio's map: a checkmark with its custom GPS position when one is written (see below), or else with the GPS position when its EXIF has one; else the place whose fallback GPS position it uses (its first place with one, or a place that place is in), with that position; else a warning that it isn't on the map. Either position ends with **Open in Google Maps**, which opens it in a new tab (`https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=lat,lng`, see [Google Maps URLs](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/urls/get-started#search-action)); the EXIF one is read from exiftool's `40 deg 46' 46.20" N, …` into decimal degrees by `parseExifGps` in `client-pipeline/src/lib/google-maps.ts`.

## Custom GPS position

The Location card's **GPS position**, under the places, shows what the map uses (the custom position or the EXIF one) and opens to a **Custom GPS position** field, written as `latitude, longitude` in decimal degrees like a place's (`40.7795, -73.9634`, which right-clicking a spot in Google Maps copies). It starts open when the EXIF has no GPS position. It's saved as `"customGpsPosition": "40.7795, -73.9634"` next to `gpsPosition`, which keeps the EXIF value, and `null` when empty; a field it can't read as a position on the globe is marked in red. The portfolio's map and the photo's info use it instead of the EXIF position, and instead of its places', and it counts as the photo's own position for the Places page's (!) warning, so a photo with one is never missing from the map. New photos get `"customGpsPosition": null`, and **Update Data → Missing fields** adds it to older ones.

> **Frontend analogy:** like `customCity`, it's a user override layered over a read-only source: `customGpsPosition ?? gpsPosition`.

## Moving the pin

The Location card (the Photo page's **Location** section, and the new photo's card under Title and comment) opens with a map of the photo's pin: red at its custom GPS position, or grey at the EXIF one. Dragging the pin, or clicking the map, writes that spot into **Custom GPS position** (six decimal places, about 10 cm), so **Save changes** saves it like a typed one. The EXIF position stays as it is and shows as a small grey dot once the pin has moved off it, and **Back to the EXIF position** (or **Remove the pin** for a photo whose EXIF has none) clears the custom one. A photo with neither starts on a map of the world, ready for a click. A position typed or pasted into the field moves the pin, and the map follows it when it lands out of view. The map is `PhotoLocationMap` (`client-pipeline/src/components/photo-location-map.tsx`), on the same CARTO basemaps and MapLibre as the Trip map.

> **Frontend analogy:** the pin is a controlled input. The map never keeps its own position: a drag's `onDragEnd` just calls the field's `onChange` with new text, and the pin is drawn from that text, so typing and dragging can't disagree.

Under the field, **This photo won't have a GPS position** marks a photo that goes without one on purpose, such as a studio photo. It's saved as `"noGps": true` (`false` otherwise, which new photos get and **Update Data → Missing fields** adds to older ones), the summary then reads **None, on purpose**, and the Dashboard's [Needs attention](../dashboard.md) leaves the photo out of its **Photos without GPS** and **Photos without a place** rows. It changes nothing on the portfolio. Save sends it next to the places and the albums and tags, not inside the sanitized EXIF, which the backend refuses with any field it doesn't know.

When the photo has no GPS position of its own (EXIF or custom), each chosen place with none either (its own or a place above it's) has **Add a GPS position for …** under that line, which opens a `latitude, longitude` field and **Save**. It saves the place's position as the Places page does, so the status line and the Places list show it at once.

Likewise, each chosen place in no city (by itself or a place it's in) gets a way to set one. When the photo shows a city (its custom one, or else its EXIF one), it's one click: **Use Seoul as Gyeongbokgung Palace's city**. When it shows none, **Set a city for …** opens the place's **City** combobox and **Save**. Either gives the place that city, and the city's country (or the photo's) when the place has no country.

**Place** replaces the old place name text box. It's one row, or two after **Add a second place**, and each row is a relation (none, in, at, by, near, on, inside, outside, behind, in front of, across from, above, below, overlooking, from) and a combobox that picks one place. Its search matches each place's name and its saved romanization, local pronunciation and local script, so “경복” or “gyeongbok” both find Gyeongbokgung Palace. Typing a name that isn't a place offers **Add “…” as a place**, which saves it as above and chooses it. Under the rows, the places read as a phrase: “In Gyeongbokgung Palace by Geunjeongmun Gate”, or “Near Gyeongbokgung” with one place and a relation. When the second place is one the first is in, its relation menu is replaced by the place's [connecting word](places.md#connecting-words) (dashed, linking to the Places page), and the photo's own `relation` for it is kept in the file but not used, so older photos need no change. With two places, **Swap places** swaps which place is first and which second, leaving each relation where it was: “In Gyeongbokgung Palace by Geunjeongmun Gate” becomes “In Geunjeongmun Gate by Gyeongbokgung Palace”.
