# Shaping the line and snapping it to streets

A line drawn by hand is straight from point to point. To make it follow the streets:

1. **Add via points**, then click the map where the line should bend. Each click puts a via point in the stretch of the line nearest it. A hop with a line picked follows the line's track, so a via point never goes on the ride itself, but it can go on the walk from the photo to the station it got on at, or from the stop it got off at to the next photo. Drag a via point to move it, or click it to remove it. **Done adding via points** stops adding. The map numbers via points v1, v2, … in the order the route goes through them, and the list under the tools groups them under the stretch they're on ("After photo 2", "Walk from Broderick St & Francisco St to photo 3"). When a stretch's via points aren't in the order a walk would pass them, **Put in order** sorts each stretch's, nearest first from where it starts (`lib/via-tidy.ts`); none moves to another stretch. On a walk, each via point has a menu, **Auto**, **Road** or **Walkway**, and a stretch with more than one has the same menu by its name to set them all (it says **Mixed** when they differ). Road makes the stretches to and from that via point take the road path, even through grounds; Walkway keeps the foot path, even when it detours; Auto lets the snap's detour check below choose. Two ends that disagree leave the stretch between them on Auto. Changing it drops the walk's path, so it's snapped again. Its arrows move one a place earlier or later: past the next via point, or past the photo or station at the end of its stretch into the next one, so a lone via point after photo 3 moves up onto the walk from the bus stop to photo 3. Its × removes it. The photo strip on **Route** shows the same numbered squares between the two photos each via point is between, before that stretch's trip stops; pointing at one says where it is.
2. Once the straight line looks right, **Snap to streets** asks the [OpenStreetMap routing servers](https://routing.openstreetmap.de/about.html) (FOSSGIS's OSRM) for the way along the streets through every walk, bus or car segment's points and via points (and every transit hop [drawn like a car or bus](lines-ridden.md#drawing-a-transit-hop-like-a-car-or-bus)), walking on foot and buses and cars by road, and for every walk to or from a station. Train and subway hops without a line stay dashed straight lines. A walk may turn back at a via point (a via point just off the way is a there-and-back), while a bus or car can't and goes on round the block, so each request says which with OSRM's `continue_straight` rather than leaving it to the server's default. Some roads are mapped as closed to people on foot (often a national route with no pavement mapped, or with its pavements mapped as separate paths that don't join it), so the foot profile reaches a via point on one by the nearest side street and back. Each stretch of a walk, from one spot (photo or via point) to the next, is checked on its own: one more than 1.6 times (and 150 m more than) the straight line between its spots is asked for by road too (with turning back allowed), and the road's stretch is drawn in its place when it's at least 15% shorter (`routing/detour.go`). Every other stretch keeps the foot path, so a walk through grounds no car enters, such as a shrine's, stays on its paths. That costs one more request, only for walks with a stretch that detours.

   > **Frontend analogy:** it's like a fetch with a fallback endpoint, tried only when the first answer fails a sanity check, rather than racing both on every call.

3. **Save route** saves the paths with the line. Snapping alone saves nothing.

`POST /api/triplines/<day>/route` → `POST /triplines/{day}/route` takes the line, checks it as saving would, and answers `{ "line": …, "unrouted": n }`: the line with a `path` on each street segment that didn't have one, and how many got no route (they stay straight). A segment keeps its path while it's unchanged; changing its photos, mode, ends or via points drops it, and the page says how many stretches are straight again.

The routing servers ask for no more than one request a second, so `be-pipeline/internal/routing` waits between requests, across every segment and every click, however fast they come. The button is off while a snap is running. `ROUTING_URL` points the backend at another OSRM server with the same `routed-foot` and `routed-car` profiles, such as one run locally.

> **Frontend analogy:** via points are like the control points of an SVG path you drag around in a design tool, and Snap to streets is a debounced `fetch` that swaps the rough path for a precise one. The backend's one-a-second wait is a throttle on that fetch, shared by every caller.
