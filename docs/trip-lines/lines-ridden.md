# Lines ridden

Under the map, **Lines ridden** lists each train, subway, bus or transit hop of a line drawn by hand, one row for each stretch of it between photos, trip stops and the day's start: a ride from photo 1 changed at Dentetsu-Izumoshi (a trip stop on it) is two rows, "Photo 1 → Dentetsu-Izumoshi" and "Dentetsu-Izumoshi → Photo 2", each with its own line. The draft keeps each stretch's line by a leg key (`lib/hop-legs.ts`): the ID of the photo the hop starts from for its first stretch, as before hops were cut, and that ID with the stretch's number after it, such as `12+1`, for each one after a trip stop. Walks to and from the stations of a stretch that starts at a trip stop can't take via spots. A hop with no line yet looks up its lines at once: the ones of its kind that pass within 500 m of both of its ends. A transit hop of unknown kind first asks **Look for a** **Train**, **Subway**, **Bus** or **Ferry** line, and looks nothing up until one is picked, since looking for every kind at once is slow on OpenStreetMap's servers (the backend turns down `mode=transit` with a 400). Picking another kind looks again; the hop stays a transit hop. Each is a [route relation](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Relation:route), usually one direction of one line, listed with its number, color and ends. When everything found is one line (say, its two directions), it's picked by itself; otherwise pick one. When the lines found are of more than one kind, **Bus**, **Subway** and **Train** (and **Other**, such as a ferry) show only that kind. Subway covers light rail, monorails and trams. **Change line** looks again.

**Or name it yourself** names the line by hand when no source has it, such as the Super HAGI bus: type its name and **Use this name**. It's saved as `{ "id": 0, "name": "Super HAGI", "source": "named" }`, kept drawn as it was (along the streets, when it was snapped), shown on the map with its name, and has no stations to pick. A **Ropeway or gondola** hop, such as the Shin-Kobe Ropeway up to the Nunobiki Herb Gardens, looks nothing up, since no source maps them as lines; its button says **Name it** (**Rename** once named), and it's drawn straight, as it runs.

Once named, a ropeway shows **Starts at** and **Ends at**, each **At the photo** until **On the map** is pressed and the map clicked where its station is (`lib/ropeway-ends.ts`, the map pick `ropeway-start:<leg key>` or `ropeway-end:<leg key>`). The ropeway is then drawn as a straight line from one station to the other, solid like a line ridden, with a walk from the photo to the station it starts at and from the station it ends at to the next photo, which **Snap to streets** routes like any walk to a station. Each station is named **Start station** or **End station** at first, renamed in its field, and moved with **Move**; **The photo** puts that end back at the photo. It saves as the hop's `board` and `alight` beside its named line, with a two-point `path` between them, the same fields a train ridden between stations uses, so the backend and the portfolio draw it as they draw any line ridden. Renaming the ropeway keeps its stations.

> **Frontend analogy:** it's a controlled input whose value is two map clicks: the stations are state on the hop, and the line on the map is derived from them, the same way a train's track is derived from its picked stations.

While it looks, the hop says where it's asking, since a busy public server can take up to a minute (see below). When the look-up needs a map that isn't loaded yet (a rail or bus map's first download, or reading one after the pipeline starts), a progress card shows from the start what it's waiting for: "Downloading OpenStreetMap's bus map of South Korea", how much of the file it has ("120 of 310 MB · 0:45") with a bar, and then "Reading …" while the download is read into lines (see [Download progress](#download-progress)). When nothing is found, often because a photo was a walk from the line (say the beach, 15 minutes from the end of the line), **Got on near** and **Got off near** each find a station by name near that photo, the same search as a [Trip's legs](../trips.md#stations-and-routes). Picking one looks again with the station standing in for the photo, and the line then gets on or off at the stop nearest it, so the walk from the photo to the station is drawn and baked as below. **The photo** goes back to looking near the photo. The stations picked here only steer the look-up; what's saved is the line's own **Got on at** and **Got off at**.

Picking a line gets its stations or stops and sets **Got on at** and **Got off at** to the ones nearest each photo. Either can be changed to another station, such as one a walk away from the next photo, or to **At the photo**. The hop is drawn along the line's track between them, solid in the line's color, with a walk from the first photo to where it got on and from where it got off to the next photo. **Clear** goes back to the straight dashed hop, and **Save route** keeps the line, the stations and the track.

## Drawing a transit hop like a car or bus

A transit hop of unknown kind is a straight dashed line, which cuts across the map when the ride went along roads no rail or bus map has, such as a shuttle up a valley. Its row's **Draw it like** menu borrows another mode's way of being routed and drawn (`lib/transit-like.ts`, `components/trip-lines/transit-like-picker.tsx`):

- **Transit (straight, dashed)**, the default.
- **Car (along roads, solid)**: **Snap to streets** (and saving) routes it by road like a drive, and the map draws it as a solid light blue drive line.
- **Bus (along roads, dashed)**: routed by road like a bus with no line, and drawn as a dashed hop.

The hop stays a `transit` hop with `"like": "car"` or `"bus"` beside its mode, kept in the draft by its leg key (like its line) and in `segmentKey`, so a path snapped as one mode isn't reused for another. Until it's snapped, the row says to. The baked map gives its line the borrowed `mode`, so the portfolio's map and legend draw it as a drive (or a hop by bus) with nothing of their own to change (`wayMode` in `github/triplines_route.go`, `wayOf` in the pipeline). A line named by hand keeps the borrowed way; a line found in a source draws along its own track instead, and the menu is hidden while it's picked.

> **Frontend analogy:** `like` is a variant prop: the hop's data still says "transit", but the renderer and the router pick their styles and behavior from `like ?? mode`, the way a `<Button asChild>` keeps its own semantics while borrowing another element's look.

## Looking lines up on Transitland or a rail map

The menu beside **Find lines** (and beside a Trip leg's **Look up route**) picks where lines are looked up, and the choice is kept in the browser for every look-up:

- While a rail map is picked, a bus is looked up on OpenStreetMap's bus map (below) and a ferry on OpenStreetMap instead (`lib/lookup-source.ts`), since the rail maps only have trains and subways; asked directly, the backend says so. A Trip leg's **Look up route** does the same.
- **OpenStreetMap** (the default): the Overpass servers, as below. Its lines are each direction of a route, mapped by volunteers, and the public servers are often busy.
- **Transitland** ([transit.land](https://www.transit.land)): the timetables transit agencies publish as GTFS, with each route's shape and stops. A route is listed when it has a stop within 500 m of both ends (`GET /routes?lat=…&lon=…&radius=500&route_types=…`, once per end, each route once when Transitland lists two copies of a feed), its track is cut from its shape the same way as an OpenStreetMap line's, and its stops are put in order along its shape. It covers only what agencies publish: many railways, but not JR West's lines in Japan, for example, and few ferries. It needs a free API key as `TRANSITLAND_API_KEY` in the repo's `.env` (see `.env.example`), sent as the `apikey` header; without one the look-up says so. Answers are kept for a day, and the backend logs each request (`be-pipeline/internal/transit/transitland.go`; `TRANSITLAND_URL` replaces the server).
- **Japan rail map**: MLIT's railway data, [国土数値情報「鉄道データ（N02）」](https://nlftp.mlit.go.jp/ksj/gml/datalist/KsjTmplt-N02-2024.html) (2024): the track of every passenger railway in Japan, by line and operator (such as 山陰線, 西日本旅客鉄道), and its stations, with no train or subway kinds told apart and names in Japanese only. The backend downloads its zip once, the first time it's asked (`N02-24_GML.zip`, tens of megabytes; a look-up that can't wait for it says to try again in a minute), keeps its two UTF-8 GeoJSON files in `MLIT_RAIL_DIR` or else in `mlit-n02-24` beside the settings file (the `/data` volume in Docker, so it survives restarts), and reads them into one network: each point of a line's track joined to the next, lines that meet joined where they share a point, and stations of one name within 500 m joined, so a ride can change there. The look-up lists first the shortest route from near one end to near the other (each change of line counting 20 km more, so it stays on one line where it can). It may start and end on any track within 1 km of each end, each meter walked to it counting as 3 m of track, so an end between two railways starts on the one that goes there, such as 一畑電車 at 電鉄出雲市 beside JR's 出雲市. The route is named by the lines it rides, such as "山陽線 → 山陰線" for Shimonoseki to Higashi-Hagi, and then every line passing within 500 m of both ends. A route's ID holds its lines (12 bits each, up to 5), so its track is the shortest way along those lines alone, and its stops are their stations in order along them. `MLIT_RAIL_URL` replaces the download (`be-pipeline/internal/transit/mlit.go`, `mlit_data.go`, and `rail.go` and `rail_graph.go`, which both rail maps share). The data is MLIT's, under its [terms of use](https://nlftp.mlit.go.jp/ksj/other/yakkan.html).
- **Rail map (OpenStreetMap)**: any other country's railways, from OpenStreetMap's extracts on [Geofabrik](https://download.geofabrik.de), looked up the same way as Japan's rail map, for when the Overpass servers are too slow. The backend downloads Geofabrik's [index](https://download.geofabrik.de/technical.html) of its extracts once (`index-v1.json`), picks the smallest one whose outline holds both ends, such as South Korea for Seoul to Busan, or a US state such as Massachusetts (ID `us/massachusetts`, named from its ID when the index gives no name) for a ride inside one (never a whole continent; a leg crossing a border finds none), and downloads that extract once (`.osm.pbf`, hundreds of megabytes; none over 2 GB). It reads the extract three times (its rail route relations, then its railway ways, then their points and the stations), keeps railways, light rail, subways and monorails but not yards or sidings, and writes them as the same two GeoJSON files as Japan's rail map in `OSM_RAIL_DIR` or else `osm-rail/v4/<extract>` beside the settings file, then deletes the extract. Railways tagged `highspeed=yes` are kept as high-speed track, and a route counts each meter of it as 0.6 m (`highSpeedFactor`), so Seoul to Busan takes the KTX's 경부고속선 rather than the slower 경부선 beside it, which is a little shorter once the change onto the high-speed line is counted. This goes by each stretch of track, not by line, since one line can run along both: the Gyeongbu Line KTX's route relation holds the high-speed line and stretches of the old line, and counting them alike drew its track along the old line. A map read by an older version (in `osm-rail/<extract>`, `osm-rail/v2/<extract>` or `osm-rail/v3/<extract>`) is downloaded and read again the first time it's used, and the old folder removed. A line is named by the [route relations](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Relation:route) (`route=train`, `subway`, `light_rail` or `monorail`) whose track it is: their [route master](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Relation:route_master)'s name, such as **Red Line** for the routes to Ashmont and to Braintree, or else the route's name less its direction ("Red Line: Alewife => Ashmont"), in the relation's `colour`. Track several lines run along belongs to each. Only track in no route relation is named by its own name, and a name that only numbers the track, such as "Track 3", names no line: the track's names alone broke Boston's Red Line into pieces named "Red Line" and "Track 1", so it couldn't be followed as one line. Each line is shown by the English name most of its ways have; unnamed track only joins lines, so a route never lists it, and a one-line route may run along it. A route relation often leaves out a station's tracks or a short stretch, so when a picked line's own track doesn't join the two spots, its track crosses each gap along other lines' track, each meter of it counting as 4 (`offLineFactor`), as a bus's track crosses gaps between its roads. A station belongs to each line within 300 m of it. A route's ID holds up to 3 lines and, above them, its extract's place in the index, so its track and stops are looked up on the same extract. The ID stays under 2^53 so the page's JavaScript holds it exactly; with 4 lines, an extract far down the index, such as Massachusetts, gave IDs the page rounded off, so a picked line's stops couldn't be found (`be-pipeline/internal/transit/osmrail.go`, `osmrail_index.go`, `osmrail_pbf.go`). The data is © OpenStreetMap contributors, under the [ODbL](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright).
- **Bus map (OpenStreetMap)**, used for a bus while either rail map is picked (the menu calls the OpenStreetMap one **Rail and bus map (OpenStreetMap)**): the same country's bus routes, read from the same Geofabrik extract as its rail map, so one download serves both. Whichever of the two is asked first downloads the extract (one at a time, so a rail and a bus look-up don't both download it) and reads every map of that country not yet on this computer: a country whose rail map was read before this downloads its extract once more for its buses. A bus route in OpenStreetMap is a route relation (`type=route`, `route=bus`, `trolleybus` or `coach`) of the roads it runs along and its stops, unlike a railway, whose track is its own ways, so the extract is read in three passes: the bus routes, then the roads they name, then those roads' points and the stops' names (`osmbus_pbf.go`). It keeps each road once and each route with its roads and its stops in order (its `stop` members, or else its platforms, as the Overpass look-up does) in `osm-rail/bus-v1/<extract>/bus-routes.json.gz`. A bus is listed when its roads pass within 500 m of both ends, the same as the Overpass look-up, its track is the shortest way along its own roads between the points nearest each end, and its stops are its own. A route whose roads aren't all joined (a short road left out of the relation, or roads listed out of order, as many are) is followed along its roads in the order the route lists them instead, each turned to carry on from the one before; only an end over 1 km from its roads fails, saying how far off each end is. The download card shows a failed map only under a look-up that failed naming it. Each direction of a route is its own line. A line's ID holds its route relation's OpenStreetMap ID in its low 40 bits and its extract's place in the index above them (`be-pipeline/internal/transit/osmbus.go`). A bus map has only buses: asked for a train, the backend says so.

> **Frontend analogy:** a bus route is like a playlist that points at songs (roads) shared with other playlists, rather than owning its own copies. Reading the extract is like resolving those references in three `fetch` waves: the playlists, then the songs they name, then each song's details.

A line picked from Transitland is saved with `"source": "transitland"`, one from Japan's rail map with `"source": "mlit"`, one from an OpenStreetMap rail map with `"source": "osmrail"`, and one from an OpenStreetMap bus map with `"source": "osmbus"`, so its stops and track are fetched from there again; one from the Overpass servers has no `source`. The `?source=transitland`, `?source=mlit`, `?source=osmrail` and `?source=osmbus` queries below ask those.

## Download progress

The rail and bus maps take minutes to download and read the first time, and seconds to read again after each start of the pipeline, so the backend lists each map being loaded, what it's doing and how far it has got, and logs each step (`map load` in the backend's terminal; `be-pipeline/internal/transit/progress.go`). The steps, in order:

- `checking`: looking for a copy saved on this computer, which is then read (`reading`) with no download.
- `waiting`: another OpenStreetMap extract is downloading; one downloads at a time.
- `connecting`: the request for the file is sent and the server hasn't answered yet.
- `downloading`: the bytes so far, the file's size (its `Content-Length`) and the speed.
- `reading`: each pass over the extract (2 for railways, 3 for bus routes, so 5 when a country's first look-up reads both), with the bytes of the extract that pass has read, such as "Reading the bus routes (pass 3 of 5 over the 312 MB extract)".
- `saving`: writing what was read, such as "Saving 4210 bus routes along 52011 roads to …".
- `failed`: why, such as "downloading California's extract failed: couldn't reach download.geofabrik.de: …". A failed load stays listed for 5 minutes so the page can still show it.

Geofabrik's list of extracts is listed the same way while it downloads.

- `GET /api/transit/downloads` answers that list, from memory: each map's `map`, `step`, `detail` (what it's doing in a sentence), `url`, `bytes`, `total`, `rate` (bytes a second), `seconds` (the whole load), `stepSeconds` and `error`.

While a hop's or a leg's look-up runs, or after it failed, the page asks it every second (`transitDownloadsQuery`) and shows a card for each map (`components/trip-lines/map-downloads.tsx`): its title ("Downloading OpenStreetMap's bus map of California"), the stages Connect, Download, Read and Save with the ones done ticked, "412 MB of 1180 MB · 6.4 MB/s · about 2 min left", a bar filled to the share downloaded or read (pulsing when that can't be told), the step's sentence, and where the file comes from and how long it has taken; or, once it failed, why. A look-up still waits at most 100 s for its map; it then answers 503 with `"loading": true`, and the page asks again by itself (`api/transit-log.ts`, up to 40 times) instead of showing an error, so the card stays until the lines are found.

Once a look-up answers, the hop or leg says what it searched, from the lines answer's `X-Transit-Searched` header (which the BFF passes on; `transit.Client.Searched`): "Searched OpenStreetMap's Overpass servers, asked live", or for a downloaded map which one and how big, and for a bus map how many routes pass near each end, such as "Searched OpenStreetMap's bus map of South Korea (21407 bus routes, saved in …): 38 pass within 500 m of the first end and 6 within 500 m of the last". So "no line found" tells a map with no bus near one end (often a photo a walk from the road) apart from buses near both ends that are different routes (a ride with a change). An answer without the header, as from a backend started before it was added, which also looks a bus up on the Overpass servers whatever source the page asks for, says to restart the backend; so does the download card when `GET /api/transit/downloads` fails.

> **Frontend analogy:** it's an upload progress bar turned around: the long job runs on the server, and the page polls a cheap status endpoint with TanStack Query's `refetchInterval` while the real request is pending, then quietly retries the request if the server timed it out.

A rail map route that changes lines, such as "北松江線 → 大社線" from 電鉄出雲市 to 出雲大社前, is ridden as each of its lines in turn. Its track answer also lists `rides`: each line alone (its own ID, so its stops are its own), its stretch of the track, and the station it changed at, the one on either line nearest where the tracks part within 1 km (川跡 here; unnamed when none is). The hop saves them as `rides` beside its `line`, the same shape as a Trip leg's (`TripLegRide`). **Lines ridden** shows them in order, "北松江線 → change at 川跡 → 大社線", the Route map draws each along its stretch in its own color, and Commit bakes each as its own line on the portfolio map, from the station changed at before it to the one after (`be-pipeline/internal/transit/rail_rides.go`, `github/triplines_transit.go`). A route of one line, or one from Transitland or the Overpass servers, has none.

> **Frontend analogy:** it's the same component with four data providers behind it, like a `fetcher` prop that switches between REST APIs returning the same shape. The rail maps are the ones that are static files: like bundling a big JSON once and searching it in memory instead of calling an API, with the route search being a shortest-path over a graph of points, the way a maps app finds directions.

- `GET /api/transit/lines?mode=subway&from=<lng,lat>&to=<lng,lat>` answers the lines found. `mode` is `train`, `subway`, `bus` or `ferry`.
- `GET /api/transit/lines/<id>/stops` answers the line's stations or stops in order, each with its `name`, `latitude` and `longitude` (its `stop` members, or else its platforms).
- `GET /api/transit/lines/<id>/track?from=…&to=…` answers `{ "path": [...] }`. It's the shortest way along the line's mapped ways between the points on it nearest each photo, so it works whichever way the ways were drawn. It fails with 422 when the line's track doesn't join the two spots. A rail map route that changes lines also answers `"rides"`, each line in turn with its stretch of track and the station it changed at (see above).

Both ask the [Overpass API](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Overpass_API) at `overpass-api.de`, which asks for light use, so `be-pipeline/internal/transit` spaces its requests a second apart, through the same throttle as the routing servers. The public servers are often busy and answer 429, 502 or 504, so when one does, the backend waits a moment and tries the next of the [public mirrors](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Overpass_API#Public_Overpass_API_instances) (`overpass.private.coffee`, then `maps.mail.ru`), going round them twice. Each try gets 40s, and the query asks the server to stop after 20s (`[timeout:20]`; a lower value is also [less likely to be turned away](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Overpass_API/Overpass_QL#timeout:) when the server is loaded). The extra time is for a busy server's wait before it starts: even a query for the few ferry terminals around Shimonoseki took overpass-api.de 22s. The whole look-up gets 100s (the BFF waits 105s), enough for a full try on two servers. A query a server turns down as wrong (400) isn't tried again. When none answer, or the 100s run out while one is still thinking, the page says the servers are busy, which server answered last and how (such as "took too long to answer"), and to try again in a minute (503).

The lines query is kept light: near the first photo it looks only at the ways the hop's lines run on (`railway` for a train or subway, `highway` for a bus, `route=ferry` for a ferry), not the buildings and footpaths around a station, and near the second photo it looks only at those lines' own ways ([`way(r.a)(around:…)`](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Overpass_API/Overpass_QL)) instead of every way there.

> **Frontend analogy:** the old query was like `document.querySelectorAll("*")` around both photos and then filtering in JavaScript; the new one is `querySelectorAll(".rail")` around the first photo and then, for the second, only checking the children of the lines already found. Answers are kept in memory for a day, up to 200 of them, so looking up the same hop again, even after reloading the page, doesn't ask a server; restarting the backend clears them. `OVERPASS_URL`, a comma-separated list, replaces the servers asked.

> **Frontend analogy:** it's a `fetch` wrapper with retry, like TanStack Query's `retry` and `retryDelay`, that also switches to a backup CDN when the first one fails, plus a `staleTime` of a day kept on the backend.

> **Frontend analogy:** a route relation is like a named SVG `<path>` in a sprite sheet, such as `#midosuji-line`. Find lines searches the sheet for paths that pass near both photos, and picking one clips that path between them.

A hop's line is kept with the mode it was picked for. Changing the hop to another kind of travel leaves the line out, and changing it back before saving brings it back. The walks to and from the stations are straight lines; Snap to streets doesn't route them.

The page reads the day files from the [combined data store](../combined-data-store.md)'s `TRIPLINES.json` with the changes since made over it, so a saved line shows at once. It (and each Trip's page) waits until the changes waiting for Commit that the backend kept from before a reload are made over the lists too (`hooks/use-session-data.ts`), so the editors start from where the session left off rather than from the committed files, and don't miss changes that arrive a moment after the lists.

The portfolio doesn't draw the lines yet; that's the next step.
