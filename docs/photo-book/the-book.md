# The photo book's Book and List tabs

The **Book** tab has three parts, as the book makers' own software does:

- **The preview**, as large as fits, keeping the pages' shape: the book as it opens, two facing pages at a time, with **‹** and **›** at the two ends of the strip under it (so they stay put while paging) to page through, where it is (**Pages 2–3 of 12**) and the pages' names, and **Preview Mode**. Page 1 is a right page, facing the inside front cover, and the last page faces the inside back cover (both drawn plain grey).
- **Edit mode**, in the strip under the preview, lets photos be dragged from one place to another on the two pages shown: dropped on a filled place they swap, on an empty one they move, each with its caption. While it's on, the album's **Photos** drawer (the one on **Photo Pages**) takes the place of **Used page spreads**: drag a photo from it onto a photo on the pages to switch that one out, or click it to fill the first empty place there; drop a photo from the pages on it to take it off. Round **+** buttons on the pages' left edge, gutter and right edge insert a new photo page there: pick its layout and it goes into the book at that spot (made after the other photo pages on **Photo Pages**), and the preview shows it. There's no **+** inside a spread. Nothing else changes there (titles, captions and the buttons on photos don't respond); **Done editing** turns it off.
- **Blank pages:** a spread always opens left and right, so one that would start on a right (odd) page gets a blank page before it, and a book with an odd number of pages gets one at the end, as a printed book has an even number. They're drawn striped and dashed, marked **Blank page · placeholder**, take page numbers (so the pages after them change side) and count toward the page budget, but are never saved: moving pages changes where they go.
- **Used page spreads**, beside it (under it on a narrow screen): the pages and spreads in the book, in order, top to bottom, each a small preview with its page numbers and name. Click one to show it in the preview; drag one onto another to put it before it, or onto the box at the end; the arrows move it and **×** takes it out, leaving it among the ones that could be used. The panel's button collapses it to a thin rail. Its list scrolls on its own (staying in view beside the preview on a wide screen), so a long book doesn't make the page longer. Pointing at one shows an eye button that shows it in the preview, as clicking its picture does.
- **All pages and spreads**, a drawer along the bottom of the window: every photo page and spread made on **Photo Pages**, then the other pages, each a small preview, dimmed and marked **In book** when it's in. Drag one into **Used page spreads**, or its **+** adds it at the end; drag one from **Used page spreads** onto the drawer to take it out. Its heading opens and closes it. The tag dropdown beside the drawer's name, once it's open, shows only the pages and spreads with that tag, to find a group of pages again, such as "Seoul" or "maybe". Tags are set on **Photo Pages**; the drawer doesn't show each one's tags, to keep it short. The layout dropdown beside it shows only the pages and spreads with a page in that layout: the built-in and the book's own layouts in use, in the order the pickers list them, **Text pages** and **Other pages**. **Show ones in the book**, beside the dropdowns and on to start, can be turned off to list only the pages and spreads not in the book yet.

When a photo is on more than one page in the book, each of them is marked **Also on page …** on **Photo Pages**.

## Print resolution

Each photo is checked against the book's page size from **Info**: its original's pixel width over the inches it covers on the printed page, once cropped to fill its place (so a photo cropped to a narrow place prints larger than the place). Below 300 dpi, the photo is marked with its resolution in red (**170 dpi**) on **Photo Pages**, and **The book** lists every such photo in the book under the preview, with its page number and page name; clicking one shows that page. A page size in pixels has no printed size, so it isn't checked, and the Book tab says so.

> **Frontend analogy:** the places are sized by CSS, so rather than copy every layout's flexbox math into TypeScript, each place measures itself with a `ResizeObserver` against its page's sheet (the same ratio at any zoom, so a small preview measures as well as a full page) and writes its dpi to a small Zustand store, which the Book tab's list reads. `lib/photo-book-dpi.ts` has the math (`printDpi`, `lowResolution`).

## Other pages

Other pages are pages without photos: one the publisher requires, a bio page, or anything else. Each shows only its name, as a placeholder, unless it's a [generated page](#generated-pages). Type a name in the drawer and **Add other page** makes one and adds it at the end of the book; each has a bin in the drawer that removes it. They're not on **Photo Pages** and are never part of a spread.

## Generated pages

A generated page is an other page drawn from the book's own data each time it's shown, so it stays current as photos, their places and the pages in the book change. **Generate page**, in the drawer beside **Add other page**, lists the kinds and adds one at the end of the book; for now there's one kind, the **Places Index**. Like any other page, it can be dragged anywhere in the book, taken out, removed with its bin, and prints in the PDF as drawn.

The **Places Index** lists every place the photos on the pages in the book show, with no photo names: each country, then its states, cities and places, each indented under the one it's in. Each photo's country, state and city are its custom ones first, as captions use them, and its places come from the places list, each under the places it's in (a gate under its palace). A photo with a city but no state takes the state the other photos have that city in, so a photo of Kobe missing its prefecture still goes under Hyōgo; a level still missing is skipped, so a city with no state sits right under its country. A state and a city with the same English name are one entry, written as the city (Cairo, with Cairo Governorate's other cities in it), so Seoul the city doesn't sit under Seoul the province and photos with either one go together; hiding it by either name hides both. A photo with only a place name lists that name. Photos on pages that aren't in the book don't count.

Its options button (the sliders, in the drawer beside its bin) sets:

- **Heading**, which starts as "Places"; an empty one leaves the page without one, and **Show page titles** on **Info** doesn't hide it.
- **Names**, from each name's [localization](../places-and-localizations.md): **As named** (as photos and places have it), **Romanized** (Gyeongbokgung), **Romanized (local)** (Gyeongbokgung (경복궁)), **Local (romanized)** (경복궁 (Gyeongbokgung), as captions' `{placeLocalized}` writes it), **Local script** (경복궁) or **As named (local script)** (Gyeongbokgung Palace (경복궁), the name as photos and places have it with its local script after). A name whose localization has no local script is written romanized in every style but the two **As named** ones, which write it as named. Alphabetical order sorts by the name as written.
- **Abbreviate US states**, such as CA for California.
- **List down to** every place, cities, states or countries.
- **Order**: alphabetical at every level, or the order the book first shows each place.
- **Pages**: 1 to 20 pages, linked, the list running on from the last column of one page to the first of the next, as a long text does. They go into the book together, one after the other, wherever the first is put, and are numbered and printed as pages of the book (unlike a spread, they start on whichever side they fall, with no blank page added). The heading is on the first page only, with its room left empty on the others so every page's list starts level. On the last page, a red mark says when the list runs past it and how many pages it needs, and a grey one when it fits on fewer; neither prints.
- **Columns**: 1 to 3 on each page, the list flowing from one to the next.
- **Text size**: the list's text from 50% to 200% of its usual size, in steps of 5; the heading stays the same size. Larger text needs more pages, which the mark on the last page says.
- **Shrink lines to fit**: a line that would wrap onto a second line by a little, such as "Okuhagi Observatory (奥萩展望" with "台)" left hanging, is drawn just small enough to stay on one line, by up to 20%; one that needs more than that wraps at its size. It's measured on the page, so it follows the columns, the text size and the names style.
- **Places**: every entry it could show, each a button that leaves it out of this page. The entries under one left out move up a level, so leaving out California puts its cities right under the United States. The − beside an entry makes its line smaller, 5% at a time down to 50% of the list's text size, with its size and a + beside it once it's smaller; use it for a line that wraps when **Shrink lines to fit** is off or isn't enough.

Hidden places on **Photo Pages** only shorten captions, so the index still lists them.

> **Frontend analogy:** the page is derived state, like a `useMemo` over the book: only its options are saved, and the tree is rebuilt from the pages in the book on every render (`lib/photo-book-places-index.ts`), each photo contributing a path such as Japan › Shimane › Matsue › Matsue Castle that's merged into the tree, like building a file tree from a list of paths. A new kind of generated page is a new entry in `GENERATED_KINDS` (`lib/photo-book-generated.ts`) with its own body in `generated-body.tsx`. **Shrink lines to fit** measures each line as one line and sets its `font-size` in `em` to the share of its column it fits in, re-measured by a `ResizeObserver` when the column's width changes, like a fit-text hook (`use-fit-scale.ts`, `lib/photo-book-entry-sizes.ts`). Running on across pages is one CSS multi-column box as wide as all the pages side by side (`column-fill: auto`, so columns fill in order instead of balancing), each page showing its own window of it, like a carousel's track behind a viewport; the last page measures where the last line ends to say how many pages are needed (`use-pages-needed.ts`). Only the first page is in the file: `bookUnits` adds the pages it runs on to as copies with their own IDs (`lib/photo-book-generated-pages.ts`).

## Preview Mode

**Preview Mode** collapses the app's sidebar, **Used page spreads** and the drawer, and shows the preview over the whole window, with nothing around it, as large as the window lets it be. **Esc** or **Leave Preview Mode** turns it off and opens the sidebar again if it was open; the arrow keys page through the book. Opening either panel leaves it too.

> **Frontend analogy:** the drawer and **Used page spreads** share one HTML5 drag type carrying the page or spread's ID, so each is both a source and a drop target, like a sortable list fed from a palette. The preview's views are derived state: the book's order grouped one way or the other, recomputed each render rather than stored.

## Save as PDF

**Save as PDF**, above the preview, makes the book's PDF on the pipeline's backend and downloads it, named after the book (`korea-2026.pdf`). Each page of the book in its order is its own sheet of paper the book's page size from **Info**. Page 1 is a right page and they alternate from there, so a custom layout's left and right looks print as they show in the preview. Blank pages print blank, and the inside covers are left out, since they aren't pages of the book. Every photo prints from its original JPEG in R2 (`images.full`), at full resolution, never a resized WEBP, so the PDF is ready for a printer and each photo has the pixels its dpi mark counts; a photo with no original in its data falls back to its resized versions, picked at about three times the size it's drawn (close to 300 dpi). The PDF is large, since it holds every photo's original JPEG, and loading them takes longer than loading resized versions did. **Save spreads as PDF**, beside it, makes the same PDF with each two-page spread on one sheet two pages wide (`korea-2026-spreads.pdf`), as the book opens: page 1 alone on the right half, with the left half blank where the inside front cover is, then pages 2 and 3, 4 and 5, and so on, and a last left page with its right half blank. Nothing that's only for editing is printed: no "Drop a photo here", dashed outlines, empty caption or title placeholders, or dpi marks.

While it works, the button says how far it has got: "Drawing the pages…", "Starting Chrome…", "Loading photos: 38 of 90", then "Printing 72 pages…". A 72-page book takes about 20 seconds once its photos are loaded; the backend gives it up to 15 minutes. A failure shows under the button.

How it works: the page draws the book's pages out of sight, as the Book tab draws them, and sends them with the pipeline's style sheets to `POST /api/photobooks/{id}/pdf` (`internal/bookpdf`). The backend serves them to a headless Chrome at a local address, waits for every photo to load (`GET /api/photobooks/{id}/pdf/progress` answers how many have), and prints them. The browser itself loads none of the large photos, which is what made the print dialog's preview hang on a long book.

The backend uses the Chrome or Chromium installed where it runs: Google Chrome on a Mac with `pnpm dev:pipeline`, and the Chromium that the backend's Docker image installs (with Liberation and Noto fonts standing in for Helvetica and Arial, Noto Arabic for Arabic, which would otherwise print as empty boxes and unjoined letters, and Noto CJK for Japanese, Chinese and Korean, which would otherwise print as empty boxes) with `pnpm dev:docker` and on the Pi. `CHROME_PATH` points it at another one, and `CHROME_NO_SANDBOX=1` (set in the image) runs it without Chrome's sandbox, which can't start as the image's unprivileged user. Photos come from R2's public addresses, so a photo the browser could only show from the offline copy prints blank.

> **Frontend analogy:** it's Playwright's `page.goto` and `page.pdf` run by the backend, on HTML the app rendered: like snapshotting a component's `outerHTML` plus `document.styleSheets` and printing that, with `@page { size: 12.5in 10.625in; margin: 0 }` and `break-after: page` on each page. A React context tells each photo it's for the PDF, so its `<img>`'s `srcset` is only the original JPEG; the copy sent sets each one to load eagerly.

## Save as image

**PDF for Blurb**, beside them, makes the book's pages and its cover as the two PDFs Blurb takes, at Blurb's sizes from **Info** ([PDF for Blurb](cover.md#pdf-for-blurb)).

**Save as image**, in the strip under the preview, saves the two pages the preview shows as one small WEBP image, to paste into an LLM and ask for feedback on the spread. It's named after the book and its pages (`korea-2026-pages-4-5.webp`, or `korea-2026-page-1.webp` for page 1 facing the inside front cover, which is left blank as in **Save spreads as PDF**). The image is 1568 pixels on its longer side, about the most Claude takes in without shrinking it, and saved at WEBP quality 80, so a spread is usually well under half a megabyte. Unlike the PDF, each photo is its resized WEBP version that fits the image, never its original JPEG, so it's quick and small. Nothing that's only for editing is drawn, as in the PDF. A failure shows under the button.

**Save all as images**, above the preview beside the PDF buttons, does the same for every spread of the book at once, as the preview pages through them, and downloads them in one zip named after the book (`korea-2026-spreads.zip`), each image named for its pages as above. While it works, the button says how far it has got: "Drawing the pages…", "Starting Chrome…", "Loading photos: 38 of 90", then "Drawing image 4 of 37". The backend gives it up to 10 minutes.

How it works: the page draws the two pages out of sight at the book's page size and sends them, with their size in CSS pixels, to `POST /api/photobooks/{id}/spread-image` (`internal/bookpdf/image.go`, `httpapi/spreadimage.go`). The backend opens them in the same headless Chrome as the PDF, with its window the spread's size and its pixel ratio set so the spread comes out 1568 pixels wide (or tall), waits for the photos, and takes a WEBP screenshot. For **Save all as images** the page draws every spread one under another and sends them to `POST /api/photobooks/{id}/spread-images` with each image's name; the backend loads them all in one Chrome tab, takes one screenshot a spread, and zips them as they are, since a WEBP is already compressed (`GET /api/photobooks/{id}/spread-images/progress` answers how far it has got).

> **Frontend analogy:** it's Playwright's `page.setViewportSize` with a `deviceScaleFactor`, then `page.screenshot({ type: "webp" })`. Because the pixel ratio is the image's, each `<img>`'s `srcset` picks the version that fits the image, as a phone's browser picks a sharper version for its screen.

# List

The **List** tab lists the book's pages in the order they're printed, as **Book** puts them, each headed by its page number, name and layout ("Page 4 · Spotlight left Two photos on the left"). A blank page the Book tab adds is left out, but its number is skipped, so the numbers match the printed book. Under each photo page, its photos in its layout's order: the place's number, the photo's Square Thumbnail, its name and its caption's first line, and **Edit info**, which opens the photo's editor (`/exif?edit=<path>`) to fix its title, Place or anything else its caption shows. An empty place says so; an other page says it has no photos. Above the pages, how many photos are on how many pages.
