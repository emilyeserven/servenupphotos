# Instagram calendar

`/instagram` is a calendar of the photos scheduled to post, one a day. It shows three weeks, starting with this one (Sunday first), and pages a week at a time; the menu before the week buttons shows 2 to 12 weeks instead, and the choice stays after a reload (`localStorage`). Clicking an empty day picks a photo for it from the photo files, read where Update Data reads them, so photos in flight on the **Add photo data** pull request are included and marked as such (eligible photos first), and ones without a title are marked **Draft** (see [Drafts](../drafts.md)): they can be scheduled, but aren't on the portfolio; clicking a scheduled photo changes or removes it. Dragging a photo to another day moves it there, and dropping it on another photo swaps the two. The page reads the photo files with `GET /api/publish/instagram`, each with its whole JSON as `data`.

Photos set aside as [Undecided](../photos-in-flight.md#undecided) (`undecided: true`) aren't on the page at all: not in the picker or its suggestions, not on the calendar, and not under **Instagram posts**. A change not committed yet counts, so marking a photo Undecided on Photos in Flight takes it off right away; the note at the top says how many are hidden (`withoutUndecided` in `lib/social-schedule.ts`). Untitled drafts are still offered.

Each change is kept in the app as a [local edit](../combined-data-store.md#local-edits) right away: the sync button at the top right counts the photo, and `ALLPHOTOS.json` in the app shows the new date. Nothing goes to GitHub until **Commit**, which sets `socialMediaPublishDate` in each changed photo file in the same commit as `ALLPHOTOS.json`. Only that field changes in each file. (`PUT /api/publish/instagram`, which saves dates on their own, is still there but the page doesn't use it.)

> **Frontend analogy:** it's a draft kept in a client-side store (persisted to `localStorage`) instead of component state, so it survives a reload and other pages can see it; Commit is the one "Save all" request.

The calendar also shows the Instagram posts saved on photos under [**Social media posts**](social-media-posts.md): a day with one gets a check in its corner when every post that day is scheduled on Instagram, or a clock while one is still planned (with a count when there's more than one), and an empty day shows that photo faded behind its **+**. Under the calendar, **Instagram posts** lists every post in three tabs, each with its count: **Posted** (scheduled on Instagram on a day up to today, newest first), **Scheduled** (scheduled on Instagram for a day after today, soonest first, and open at first) and **Unposted** (still planned, whatever the day, soonest first; `postsByTab` in `lib/social-post-tabs.ts`). Each post shows its photo, the tab's name, whether the photo is **In flight** or as the **Gallery** has it, a link to the post and a link to edit the photo. Gallery and in flight photos are both included, and nothing more is fetched for them: the posts come from the photo files the calendar already read, with the changes waiting for Commit and local edits the app keeps (`useChangedPhotoFiles` in the combined data store) made over them, so a post saved on the Photo page shows here before **Commit**. `apps/client-pipeline/src/lib/social-post-days.ts` does the merging (`postedPhotoFiles`, `postsByDay`), for any page that needs each day's posts.

> **Frontend analogy:** it's an optimistic UI: the list is the last server response with the store's unsent mutations replayed over it, rather than a refetch.

## Social Feed preview

The page has two tabs under the Focus: **Calendar** (the calendar and **Instagram posts**) and **Social Feed**. The open tab is kept in the address as `?tab=feed`, so a reload or a link opens the same one. On the **Social Feed** tab, **Social Feed preview** shows the Instagram profile grid as it will look at the end of a chosen day: three columns of 3:4 tiles, newest first, like the profile page. The day starts as today; pick another with the date picker to see the feed on any day before or after, step a day at a time with the arrow buttons on either side of it or the left and right arrow keys (unless a field, menu or the tabs have focus), and **Today** goes back. The tiles are every photo on the calendar (with dates moved and not committed yet) and every Instagram post with a day under **Social media posts**, up to that day; a photo that is on the calendar and has a post on the same day is one tile. Photos that post on the chosen day are ringed, and ones still to come after today get a corner mark: a calendar for a calendar day, a clock for a planned post, and a check for one scheduled on Instagram. Each tile links to its Photo page, and the grid shows four rows at a time with **Show more** for the rest. A tile whose photo is on the calendar can be dragged onto any other tile, in any row, to move it to that tile's day, swapping with the calendar photo already there, just as dragging on the calendar does (they share the same drag, so a tile can also be dropped on a calendar day). Tiles that come only from **Social media posts** can't be dragged, since their day is the post's, set on the Photo page. `apps/client-pipeline/src/lib/social-feed.ts` builds the feed (`feedEntries`, `feedAsOf`).

## Grid Anchors

One photo on the calendar can be the **Grid Anchor**, set from its day's picker (**Make Grid Anchor**, next to **Remove from this day**), to plan thematic rows of the feed. The anchor and the 2 calendar photos just before it make one row of the 3-column feed (`ANCHOR_ROW` in `apps/client-pipeline/src/lib/grid-anchors.ts`), and every other calendar photo falls into rows of 3 counted from it: back from it for earlier photos, on from it for later ones (`anchorPlaces`). There's only ever one: making another photo the anchor moves it there, which shifts every row, and **Remove Grid Anchor** on it takes the rows away. (If more than one photo is marked, such as from an older save, the latest on the calendar is the anchor.)

Rows are outlined dark blue and light blue by turns, on the calendar and in the **Social Feed preview**. The last photo of each row gets an anchor badge: solid in its row's color on the Grid Anchor itself, faint on the ones worked out from it. The outlines are only a reference: the feed doesn't flag a row that isn't lined up, since rows drift in and out of line in a cycle.

While there's an anchor, a day's picker also takes an optional **Group name** for that photo's row, shown on the badge of the row's last photo. The name is saved on one photo of the row (the newest that already has one, or else the picked photo), so it moves with its photos when the anchor shifts the rows.

The anchor is saved as `socialGridAnchor: true` and a name as `socialGridGroupName` on the photo's JSON, kept as local edits until **Commit** like a moved date; photos without them leave the fields out.

## Not Stored days

Sometimes something goes up on Instagram whose photo isn't in the portfolio. An empty day's picker has **Mark as Not Stored**, which fills that day with a dashed **NOT STORED** box instead of a photo. Clicking it opens the day's own dialog, with an optional note on what was posted (such as "Story repost") and **Remove Not Stored**, which frees the day again. A Not Stored day can't take a photo, so a photo dropped on it stays where it was; remove it first.

A Not Stored day counts like a photo everywhere the feed's shape matters:

- It takes a tile in the **Social Feed preview** on its day, marked **NOT STORED** with its note, which links nowhere and can't be dragged.
- It counts toward the rows of 3 worked out from the [Grid Anchor](#grid-anchors), and is outlined with its row. It can't be the Grid Anchor itself or hold its row's name; those stay on photos.
- It counts as a day scheduled on Instagram for the Dashboard's [Needs Attention](../dashboard.md) check of the next 7 days, since it's on Instagram already.

Each is one file, `notstored/<day>.json` (`{ "id": "2026-11-10", "day": "2026-11-10", "note": "" }`), saved as a [change waiting for Commit](../pending-changes.md) (`PUT /api/notstored/<day>` and `DELETE /api/notstored/<day>`), and **Commit** combines them into `notstored/NOTSTORED.json`, as it does caption templates. In the browser, a Not Stored day stands in for a photo with the path `not-stored:<day>` (`apps/client-pipeline/src/lib/not-stored.ts`), so the Grid Anchor's rows and the feed can count it without a photo file.

> **Frontend analogy:** it's a placeholder item in a list, like a skeleton card that holds a slot in a CSS grid so the items after it don't shift, except this one is saved.
